## Overview

Food and organic waste together form the largest single component of many municipal waste streams. When edible food is wasted it exacerbates food insecurity and when organics are landfilled they produce methane, a powerful short-term greenhouse gas. This combined model prioritizes "people-first" recovery of edible food, then safe collection and treatment of residual organics so nutrients return to local soils.

## Goals & Metrics
- Diversion target: X% of edible food recovered and Y% of organics diverted from landfill within 3 years.
- Processing capacity: Permitted composting/AD capacity to match projected diverted tonnage.

## 1. Food Recovery & Redistribution (Edible Food First)

### Rationale
Preventing edible food waste reduces emissions and strengthens local food security. Donation must be mandated and de-risked through legal protections.

### Key Measures
- Liability protection / Good Samaritan provisions for donors.
- Procurement rules for public institutions to prioritize redistribution of surplus.
- Partnerships with food banks and social enterprises; grants for logistics and storage.

## 2. Mandatory Source Separation & Collection

### Household & Commercial Separation
- Require separation of Organic Materials (food scraps, soiled paper, yard trimmings) from landfill waste.
- Provide standardized "Brown Bins", vented kitchen caddies, and starter supplies (compostable liners where appropriate).

### Unit-Based Pricing and Incentives
- Implement PAYT (weight or volume-based fees) to align household costs with waste generation.
- Offer subsidized or free organic collection for low-income households to avoid regressive impacts.

## 3. Processing & Infrastructure

### Composting & Anaerobic Digestion
- Prioritize siting of decentralized composting and AD facilities to minimize haul distances.
- Require municipal parks and public lands be primary recipients of city-generated compost to close the nutrient loop.

### Quality & Health Controls
- Establish contamination thresholds and testing protocols for compost product quality.

## 4. Enforcement, Phasing & Support

### Phased Rollout
- Phase 1: Target large generators (supermarkets, institutions) and ensure processing capacity.
- Phase 2: Scale to businesses and high-density residential areas.
- Phase 3: Full residential rollout with education-first enforcement.

### Technical Support
- Provide technical assistance, procurement templates, and small grants for small businesses.

## 5. Implementation Checklist

- [ ] Waste characterization and processing capacity audit
- [ ] Procurement of bins and kitchen caddies for pilot zones
- [ ] Pilot: commercial diversion and high-frequency organics collection
- [ ] Residential rollout and contamination management

## Case Studies

South Korea (RFID PAYT), Vermont (Act 148), Milan (high collection rates with vented pails) and San Francisco (large-scale municipal composting) provide real-world models for the phased approach described above.
